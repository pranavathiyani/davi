DAVI is an interactive browser for the EvE Bio drug-target-activity dataset: a quantitative screen of 1,397 mostly-FDA-approved drugs against nuclear receptors, GPCRs, and protein kinases. Each network shows what a compound hits, or what hits a target, with edge thickness scaled by potency (pXC50) and dashed orange edges flagging cytotoxicity concerns. Using DAVI

What you're looking at

DAVI shows ego networks from the EvE Bio drug-target-activity dataset. Each visualization centers on one compound or one target. Surrounding nodes are everything it interacts with. Edge thickness = potency (pXC50). Edge color = clean (grey) vs. flagged for viability concerns (dashed orange).

What pXC50 means

pXC50 is the negative log of the concentration at which 50% of maximum activity is reached (i.e., pIC50 or pEC50, depending on assay mode). Higher = more potent. The data only quantifies pXC50 in the range tested (10 pM to 10 μM), so the minimum quantified value is 5.0 and the maximum is around 11. Active compounds outside this range are flagged but not quantified, and we call those weak hits.

  • pXC50 < 6: weak interaction, ~μM range
  • pXC50 6–7: moderate, sub-μM to 100 nM
  • pXC50 > 8: strong, <10 nM, pharmacologically meaningful

Flags to watch

viab Compound caused >15% cell viability loss in the corresponding viability assay. Antagonist-mode 7TM hits with viab flags may be cell death masquerading as antagonism.

freq Compound hit suspiciously often within a target class / mode (>20 hits). May be a promiscuous binder or assay-interference compound. Filter or interpret carefully.

Controls

  • Search box: type any compound name, gene symbol, UniProt, DrugBank, or CAS. Arrow keys to navigate results, Enter to select.
  • pXC50 cutoff slider: hides edges weaker than the threshold.
  • Show top N: caps the visible network at N most-potent neighbors. Useful for hub targets.
  • Click any node: re-centers the network on that compound or target.
  • Drag a node: pulls it briefly, springs pull it back to equilibrium.

Target classes

  • 7TM: G-protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). Cell-based assays, two modes (Agonist/Antagonist) and two signaling pathways (G-protein, β-arrestin2).
  • NR: Nuclear receptors. Cell-free biochemical TR-FRET. Agonist + Antagonist.
  • Kinase: Protein kinases. Cell-free competitive binding. Includes some clinically-important mutant variants.
